# Messaging Pillars, Voice & Tone

## Pillars (every major content piece should map to at least one)

1. **Show the work.** We publish the verdict logic, the exclusion rules, and the public record.
   Confidence comes from transparency, not promises.
2. **Model over gut.** Every number traces back to a stated methodology (weights, VORP,
   backtests) — never "our experts think."
3. **One board, every use case.** Betting and fantasy aren't separate products bolted together —
   same modeling discipline, one subscription.
4. **Honest about uncertainty.** A PASS or TRAP verdict is proof the system says "no" — lean into
   this instead of hiding it.

## Voice

- Direct, confident, a little dry. Sports-bar-with-a-spreadsheet, not hype-bro.
- Numbers do the talking — lead with the stat, not the adjective.
- Short sentences. No exclamation-point stacking.
- It's fine to be plain about downside: "PASS means no edge today" is more trustworthy copy than
  pretending every day is a green day.

## Tone by channel

| Channel | Tone |
|---|---|
| X/Twitter (picks bot + brand account) | Terse, confident, numbers-first. Matches the in-app AI chat's own style guide: "under 200 words... no fluff." |
| Email digest | Warm but efficient — one clear recommendation, skimmable in under 30 seconds. |
| Landing page | Confident hero claim, then immediately back it with the live record stat strip. |
| In-app / product copy | Plain, functional, no marketing voice — this is a tool, not an ad. |

## Words to use

edge, verdict, model, tracked, public record, tiered, backtested, on the clock, roster need

## Words to avoid

"guaranteed," "lock," "can't lose," "insider," anything implying certainty of outcome. This is a
probabilistic model on stochastic events — copy should never imply otherwise. See
`proof-points.md` for the compliance-adjacent language rules around win-rate and "beat Vegas"
claims.
